# Aixel Labs — Social content brand brief (paste this into any AI agent)

**Scope:** this file governs *social media posts* (X/Twitter, Instagram, Reddit) —
marketing content made to be posted externally. It does not override
`BRAND.md`, which remains the source of truth for in-app product UI.
Logo assets are the same: see `assets/` and `BRAND.md` §2.

**Why this file exists:** generic "AI content" prompts and skills produce a
recognizable look and voice — see §0. This file exists so posts about Aixel
Labs never read as generated.

---

## 0. What to actively avoid (researched, not guessed)

Before writing this brief, we pulled ~40 currently-ranking YouTube videos
about "AI social media content skills/prompts" through Aixel Labs' own
YouTube intelligence tooling and read transcripts of the top ones. Three
patterns are already saturating the space:

| Pattern | What it actually is | Why we're not doing it |
|---|---|---|
| **"Skills for social media"** (hook generator, thumbnail A/B, caption funnel, chained to Higgsfield/Nano Banana) | An operational content-generation pipeline | Produces volume, not identity — every account using it sounds the same |
| **"Claude Design" workflow** (generate a design system from one photo, reuse it for a website + menu + IG posts) | A generic-first-draft generator, by the maker's own admission in the walkthrough | The "aha, no more AI slop" framing undersells that pass one is still generic — it's a starting point, not a finished identity |
| **"De-slop skill"** (checks AI output against a vague "quality bar," flags misaligned copy) | A QC gate | It's a filter, not a style — it tells you *no*, never *what instead* |

**The irony worth naming out loud:** the visual look most AI tools now
converge on — warm cream/paper background, a serif display headline, a
terracotta/rust accent — was itself popularized by workflows like "Claude
Design." It reads as hand-crafted the first time you see it and as
templated the tenth. Do not adopt cream-paper-serif-terracotta as if it
were the "authentic, non-slop" choice. It is the 2026 equivalent of the
purple-gradient-blob SaaS hero from 2023 — recognizable as generated,
not as considered.

**What we do instead:** keep Aixel Labs' own established identity (violet
`#4f46e5`/`oklch(0.606 0.25 292.717)`, the "A." mark, Inter) as the anchor,
and get distinctiveness from **restraint and specificity** — real product
screenshots, real numbers, plain sentences — not from a swapped-in surface
palette.

## 1. Voice

Write like an operator talking to other operators, not a brand account
narrating itself.

**Do**
- Lead with one concrete number or a before/after: *"480 leads scraped from
  Google Maps in 12 minutes, no manual copy-paste."*
- Say "I/we built," admit limits: *"LinkedIn people search is still behind a
  flag. Company search is solid."*
- One idea per post, one idea per carousel slide, one idea per tweet.
- Show the actual product — lead cards, the credits counter, real search
  results — never stand-in art.

**Don't**
- Hype words: *unlock, supercharge, game-changing, 10x, in minutes!!*
- Emoji strings as punctuation (🚀🔥✨) or as bullet markers.
- Hook-formula openers lifted from creator "hook generator" skills —
  *"One thing that cut my X from 50 hours to 20"* is now a template, not a
  voice. If a line could headline any SaaS launch, cut it.
- Opening a Reddit post like a press release ("In today's fast-paced
  digital landscape...").

## 1a. Grammar and mechanics — no AI tells

This is the part that matters most in Reddit comments and replies (least
scripted, most scrutinized for being a bot) but applies everywhere copy
gets typed by a human, not a template.

**Punctuation tells — cut these**
- The em dash / double-hyphen as a clause joiner (`—`, `--`). AI writing
  leans on it to fuse two thoughts that should just be two sentences.
  Use a period. Or a comma. Or start over.
- Semicolons joining two clauses. Nobody texts with a semicolon.
- A colon followed by a tidy list, inside a caption or comment. That's
  slide-deck punctuation, not conversation.
- Symmetric "not just X, it's Y" or "not only X but also Y" constructions.
  They scan as generated because a real person rarely balances a sentence
  that evenly.
- **Correctly hyphenated compound modifiers** — *people-search, real-time,
  data-driven, AI-powered.* A person typing fast doesn't stop to
  hyphenate. They write "people search," "real time," "data driven," or
  skip the compound entirely. Precise hyphenation is copy-editor
  behavior, not texting behavior — treat every compound modifier as a
  tell, same as the em dash.

**Word tells — cut these**
> practically, essentially, fundamentally, arguably, notably, undeniably,
> seamlessly, effortlessly, meticulously, meticulous, delve, boast,
> tapestry, testament, realm, landscape, elevate, harness, leverage,
> unlock, unparalleled, cutting-edge, robust, intricate, vibrant,
> "in today's fast-paced world," "it's important to note," "in conclusion"

If a word only ever shows up in a listicle or a press release, it doesn't
belong in a reply either.

**Write it plain — closer to how you'd actually type**
- Short sentences over long constructed ones. Contractions always —
  *didn't, can't, wasn't* — never *did not, cannot*.
- Plain, direct phrasing over polished phrasing. Closer to a WhatsApp
  reply than a brochure — this is a Pune-built product, the voice can
  sound like it: direct, a bit blunt, "actually," "only," "no issues,"
  skip the throat-clearing intro, get to the point.
- Occasional CAPS on exactly *one* word for emphasis — like typing while
  mildly annoyed or genuinely amused — not a fully capitalized phrase, and
  not every other post.

**Don't hit shift for every sentence**
Real replies don't stop, capitalize, and restart for every thought. They
run on with "and," "so," "but," or just a comma, and often skip
capitalizing whatever comes next. Save the full stop + capital letter for
when a genuinely separate thought starts — otherwise let it run, the way
an actual reply looks when someone typed it on their phone in twenty
seconds, not the way a sentence looks when it was constructed to be
grammatically complete.

**Let it be a little wrong**
A dropped letter, a transposed one, a missing apostrophe, a word that
trails off unfinished, an "i" that never got capitalized — that's what
real fast typing looks like, not a defect to fix. It's the strongest
signal available, because it's the one thing a copy-polishing pass always
removes first.

- **Comments are where this matters most, not posts.** A post is a
  headline — a little composure there is fine. A comment reply is what
  people actually scrutinize, and it's what most people read anyway. A
  slightly rough comment builds more trust than a slightly rough post.
- Use it sparingly — one small slip per comment, not five. A comment
  where every sentence has a typo reads as trying to look human, which is
  its own kind of fake.
- Don't make it a formula. Always dropping the same letter, always
  writing "teh" for "the," reads as a bit, not a person. Vary it, or skip
  it entirely most of the time.

**Humor — use it, but only if it's actually about the post**
Humor is what makes a reply get upvoted instead of scrolled past. The
rule: the joke has to be about the specific feature or the specific
failure being discussed — never a generic one-liner bolted onto the top.
A forced joke reads as fake faster than a hype word does.

- *"LinkedIn people search is still behind a flag. Think of it as a
  secret menu item, except the secret is 'not done yet.'"* A pun about
  the actual limitation, not a generic joke.
- Self-deprecating > self-congratulating. A joke about where the product
  breaks lands better than a joke about how great it is.
- If there's no honest angle for a joke, skip it. Silence beats a forced
  pun.

**Before / after — same fact, three voices**

> ❌ *"Our platform seamlessly integrates Google Maps and Advanced Search,
> practically eliminating the need for manual data entry — it's not just
> a tool, it's a game-changer for lead generation."*

> ✅ *Post* — "it pulls from maps and search together so you're not
> copying stuff between two tabs anymore. built it mostly because i was
> tired of doing that myself at 1am. still breaks on linkedin people
> search, thats on me for now."

> ✅ *Comment reply* — "ya maps + search running together is the main win
> here, linkedin people serach is still off tho, only company search
> works rn"

The post version drops the em dash, the hyphenated compound, and most
capitalization — it runs on instead of stopping and restarting for every
thought. The comment version goes further: lowercase throughout, "rn"
for "right now," and one natural typo ("serach") nobody would plant on
purpose. Neither needed a single banned word to say what the first line
took three clauses and a hype phrase to say.

## 2. Visual system for social-only assets

This is a layer on top of the app brand (`BRAND.md` §3), not a
replacement. Same violet, same "A." mark — applied with more restraint
than in-product UI because a social asset has one job, not a dashboard's
worth.

| Token | Value | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas (pick one per asset, never mix) | Near-black `#0B0A10` **or** clean off-white `#FAFAFA` | No gradients, no glass, no cream/paper tint |
| Accent | Aixel violet, `oklch(0.606 0.25 292.717)` (~`#7C5CFC`) | One precise use per asset — an underline, a pill, a 1px border. Never a full-bleed wash or hero gradient |
| Type | Inter — 700 for the one claim, 400 for support | Numbers in tabular/mono style when showing metrics — signals "real number," not "vibe" |
| Imagery | Real product screenshots, cropped tight, thin 1px frame | When no screenshot fits: one flat-line icon, single stroke weight. Never stock photos of robots, glowing brains, or people pointing at holograms |
| Mark | "A." mark, small, one corner | Treat it like a maker's stamp, not a hero lockup |
| Motion (Reel covers etc.) | None of the in-app `animate-ai-*` shimmer/pulse utilities | Those read as "AI is thinking" in-product; on a static social asset they read as noise |

## 3. Platform playbooks

### X / Twitter
- **Primary format:** plain text thread, no image, 4–7 tweets, one idea per
  tweet. Text-only threads read as a person, not a graphic-design account.
- **When you do use an image:** 1600×900, one canvas color, one real
  screenshot crop, one line of bold Inter text (≤8 words), "A." mark in the
  corner, 24px margin.
- **Hook line test:** state the mechanism or the number, not a formula.
  *"We scrape Maps and Google Search without you opening either tab"* beats
  *"This ONE tool will change how you find leads forever."*
- Reply and quote-tweet in first person — not a corporate "we appreciate
  your feedback!" voice.

### Instagram
- Grid alternates two modes only: **(a)** screenshot-forward, thin-frame
  crop, or **(b)** a rare single-claim post on flat canvas. No listicle
  carousels padded past 4 slides.
- **Carousel** (only for an actual multi-step walkthrough): 1080×1350, max
  4 slides. Slide 1 = one claim + one screenshot. Slides 2–3 = one concrete
  step or number each, no bullet stacking. Slide 4 = plain CTA, no arrow
  emoji, no "SWIPE ➡️."
- **Caption formula:** one concrete sentence of what happened → 1–2
  sentences of detail or a number → plain CTA. Max 3 hashtags, exact-match
  only (`#leadgen #b2bsales`), never a hashtag block.

### Reddit
- **No visual asset.** Reddit is text-native; a designed card reads as an
  ad and gets removed or downvoted on sight.
- Post from an account with real history, in the right subreddit
  (r/SaaS, r/sales, r/Entrepreneur, niche B2B subs), and follow that
  community's self-promo rule (often a 90/9/1 ratio, or self-promo-only on
  specific days — check each subreddit's rules before posting).
- **Structure:** *"I built X because Y. Here's exactly what it does and
  where it still breaks."* Disclose that you're the builder up front.
  Invite criticism. Answer every comment yourself, in your own voice.
- Never cross-post identical copy to multiple subreddits. Never link-drop
  without context.

## 4. Reusable prompt templates (paste into Claude, adapt the bracket)

**A — X post**
> Write one X post about [feature/result] for Aixel Labs. Lead with the
> specific number or mechanism, not a hype claim. One sentence, under 220
> characters, no emoji, no hashtags. Then suggest one real product
> screenshot to pair it with, and say why that crop.

**B — Instagram carousel outline**
> Outline a max-4-slide Instagram carousel about [feature/result] for
> Aixel Labs. Slide 1: one claim + which screenshot to show. Slides 2–3:
> one concrete step or number each, no bullet stacking. Slide 4: plain
> CTA, no arrow emoji. Flag any line that sounds like a hook-formula
> opener and rewrite it plainer.

**C — Reddit draft check**
> Draft a Reddit post for r/[subreddit] introducing [feature] as its
> builder — first person, plain sentences, disclose the build, invite
> critique, no marketing language. Then re-read the draft and list which
> lines would read as AI-generated to a Reddit regular, and rewrite those.
