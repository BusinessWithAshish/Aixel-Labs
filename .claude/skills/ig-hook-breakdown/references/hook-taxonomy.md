# Hook & content taxonomy

Rubric for `ig-hook-breakdown`. Assembled from the concrete, verified
mechanics found across the strongest builds in the Instagram-analysis-skill
research (global + India passes) — not paraphrased marketing copy, the
actual extracted prompts/rubrics/formulas.

## 1. Hook trigger taxonomy (10 psychological triggers)

Use this closed set — don't invent new categories. A hook can lean on more
than one, but call out the dominant one.

1. **Curiosity** — makes the viewer feel they're missing something
2. **Loss frame** — what they lose by not watching/reading further
3. **Contrast** — what most people think vs. what's actually true
4. **Specificity** — a precise number/date/detail that signals credibility
5. **Controversy** — a claim that splits the audience (half nod, half lean in)
6. **Pattern interrupt** — starts mid-thought, or breaks the expected opening
   structure for the format/niche
7. **Identity** — speaks directly to who the viewer sees themselves as
8. **Social proof** — anchors the claim in what others are already doing
9. **Future pace** — puts the viewer in a moment after they've acted
10. **Simplicity** — strips everything away, states the core truth in one line

**Generation constraints** (apply when producing new hooks, not when
classifying existing ones): each hook is one or two short sentences max; none
can open with "I" or "Are you"; none can end with a question mark; when
generating a batch, all must be genuinely different triggers, not variations
on one structure. Close a generated batch with a self-ranked pick: "Strongest
hook for this audience: [n] — [one-line why]."

## 2. Hook tests

A hook should pass all three. Note which it fails, don't just give a vague
score.

- **Fear/curiosity/identity-relevance test** — does the opening create fear
  of loss, strong curiosity, or identity relevance? If it's just a neutral
  statement of topic, it fails.
- **Pattern-interrupt test** — could you point to the exact visual movement,
  facial expression, or verbal contradiction in the first 1–2 seconds that
  would stop a scroll? If the answer is "nothing in particular," it fails.
- **Stranger test** — would this make sense to someone with zero context on
  this account, within 3 seconds, with no prior knowledge? If it needs
  the viewer to already follow the account or know the series, it fails.

## 3. Hook anatomy (first ~5 seconds)

Break the hook zone into three layers when material is available:

- **Visual** (first 1–2s): movement, facial expression, contrast, pattern
  break — note if unavailable (audio-only transcript, no frame analysis)
- **Text** (on-screen caption/overlay, if inferable): should be short —
  danger, promise, or contradiction stated in as few words as possible
- **Audio** (first spoken words): should read as confident and immediate —
  no greeting, no throat-clearing intro ("hey guys," "so today")

## 4. Retention mechanisms (across the full transcript, not just the hook)

Look for these by name, with rough timestamps where they occur:

- **Open loops** — explicit deferred payoff ("in a second I'll show you why...")
- **Delayed payoff** — the actual answer/result is held back from the reward
- **Micro-escalations** — new information or stakes introduced roughly every
  3–5 seconds, not one flat delivery
- **Pattern interrupts mid-video** — pauses, zooms, gesture changes, scene
  cuts that reset attention
- **Forward momentum** — each line implies there's more coming, avoids
  sounding like a closed statement

## 5. Script structure (Hook → Problem → Agitation → Solution → Proof → Offer → CTA)

Map the transcript onto these seven stages. Most organic (non-ad) content
uses a subset — note which stages are present vs. skipped, don't force all
seven onto every reel.

1. **Hook** — the opening pattern interrupt (see above)
2. **Problem** — what pain/question is being named
3. **Agitation** — why the problem matters more than the viewer assumed
4. **Solution** — the mechanism/system/method being presented (what's the
   actual "system" being sold — e.g. "a shortcut method," "a root-cause
   fix," "a secret formula" — name the specific mechanism, not just "advice")
5. **Proof** — credibility signal: social proof, authority, specificity,
   before/after
6. **Offer** — what's being asked of the viewer to get the result (may be
   implicit for organic content — e.g. "follow for part 2")
7. **CTA** — comment/save/follow/link-in-bio/DM-keyword, and where it sits
   (early/mid/late)

When scoring persuasion-heavy content (ads, sales-adjacent reels), also name
the specific **psychological trigger** at play using standard terms: social
proof, authority bias, loss aversion, curiosity gap, fear, transformation/
identity-shift — and say *how* each is being used, not just that it's present.

## 6. Reward type

Classify the dominant payoff:

- **Education** — payoff is clarity/understanding
- **Entertainment** — payoff is emotional release (humor, catharsis, drama)
- **Inspiration** — payoff is self-belief or a prompt to act

## 7. Style DNA checklist (cross-reel synthesis only)

When synthesizing patterns across 3+ reels from one account, extract these
fields specifically — this is a "how it works" checklist, not a "what it's
about" summary:

- Niche / target audience
- Hook style (which trigger(s) recur most)
- Script flow (which of the 7 stages appear most consistently, and in what
  order)
- Sentence rhythm (short punchy lines vs. longer explanatory ones)
- Tone
- Transitions (how the account moves between ideas/scenes)
- Curiosity gaps (how open loops are typically phrased)
- Emotional triggers (which recur)
- Retention techniques (which of section 4's mechanisms show up most)
- Direct address (does the creator speak to "you" directly, how often)
- Words per second (rough pacing)
- Average word count per reel, and implied target range

Rule: **do not summarize the topic** ("this account posts about skincare").
Extract the mechanism ("this account opens on a contrarian claim about a
common skincare habit, holds the payoff until ~8s, and closes every hook
under 6 words"). If the extracted pattern would apply to swapping in a
different topic entirely, it's a real style signal; if it wouldn't survive a
topic swap, it's topic description, not style — discard it here.
